# Art direction — the redesign

*Paintings since 2019*, second edition of the book. 163 pages become 129.
This file is the argument; `GUIDE.md` is the binding specification;
`book.py` is the only place a page is made.

## 1 · The statement

The paintings are assembled bodies: cells leaning on cells, decisions taken
in wet paint, large grounds left bare and carrying weight. The first edition
answered that with an archive — one loop of title page, plate, detail sheet,
grid, contact sheet, run thirty-five times. Correct, and inert. Page 24
proved it: two good crops floating in leftover white, each on its own page,
saying nothing to each other.

The second edition is built on **controlled rupture**. One modernist grid
runs under every page — twelve columns, a 5 mm baseline, four horizontal
registers — and it is broken on purpose, a few times per sequence, by scale,
by a crop crossing the spine, by a sentence set at the size of a painting.
The grid is the discipline; the painting is the rupture; neither imitates
the other. Every composition is a spread before it is a page: one dominant
element per spread, one deliberate relation between left and right, white
that answers the mass it faces rather than remaining after it.

The unit of editing is the argument, not the inventory. A detail is printed
because it stands in tension with another image on the same spread — face
against face, early mark against final form, source against outcome — and
each exact crop is printed once in the book. What returns, returns changed:
wider, located, opposed, or at another scale. The reading direction of the
book (newest first) is kept and made physical: 2026 is dense with process,
2023 is one painting inside a visible silence, 2020 tightens, 2019 opens
into the archive of where it all began.

## 2 · The structure — 129 pages

| pp. | section |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Cover · imprint · half-title |
| 4–5 | *On the work* — the artist's text facing a full-bleed detail |
| 6–7 | Biography · exhibitions |
| 8–9 | How the book is arranged · contents |
| 10–43 | **2026** — six works, 34 pp.: openings, detail arguments, process sequences (Leonardo → Virgil; the Soprano still → gary grills cooper) |
| 44–45 | **2023** — the gap: one commission on one spread, the silence stated |
| 46–73 | **2020** — ten works, 28 pp.: two paired spreads, the versions of *ciao capo*, one typographic pause |
| 74–111 | **2019** — eighteen works, 38 pp.: five paired spreads, plate-crop readings, the studies, one pause |
| 112–113 | Index — the thirty-five at one width |
| 114–127 | *What comes back* — opening + six motif spreads, each with its own behaviour |
| 128–129 | Colophon · the cover located on the painting it was cut from |

Every work unit is a whole number of spreads and every work opens on a
verso, so a spread is always one composition. Year thresholds are not
section pages: the year enters as a large numeral on the opening spread of
the first work of that year, and the 2023 spread is itself the account of
the gap.

## 3 · What is merged or cut (163 → 129)

1. **The 35 standalone plate pages** — merged with their text pages into
   one opening spread each. No painting floats centred in a band with dead
   white above and below; plates top-align on the first register and scale
   with the physical size of the canvas (100 × 160 cm prints monumental,
   29.7 × 42 cm prints small — the honesty is the hierarchy).
2. **The reprise system** ("the same place, whole") — cut. Fourteen exact
   duplicate crops leave the book.
3. **The echo system** (° crops printed in the work section *and* the
   recurring chapter) — cut. Motif crops now appear only in *What comes
   back*; a revisit is a different crop or a wider view by rule.
4. **Eight-cell detail grids** (pp. 13, 21, 30, 39, 45 of the first
   edition) — replaced by curated arguments of one dominant and at most
   three counter-images. Surplus crops that repeat information are cut
   (about 30 of 84 crops).
5. **Equal-thumbnail process contact sheets** (pp. 14–15, 26–28, 36–37,
   47, 56) — 49 stages reduced to the 26 that show a decision, set as one
   dominant moment plus a strip.
6. **The interleaf filler pages** ("Recurring here", "From the note") —
   structural parity makes them unnecessary; all nine leave.
7. **The index display page** (p. 137) — the index opens directly.
8. **Six identical recurring-section text pages + six identical grids**
   (pp. 150–161) — replaced by six spreads, each with its own logic (§ the
   families, below).
9. **The duplicated quote-page template** (pp. 71 / 141) — each pause is
   now composed once, with the image its sentence refers to.
10. **Study sheets with floating thumbnails** (pp. 133–135, 140) — studies
    are set as temporal comparisons beside what they became, or docked to
    the opening's text column.

Nothing factual leaves: all 35 works, every title in the artist's spelling,
dimensions, media, places, the three notes per work, the essay, biography,
exhibitions, the six motif texts, index and colophon are all kept.

## 4 · Five spread families

All five share the grid (12 columns, registers at 18 / 83 / 148 / 213 mm);
none is a fixed template. Family repetition is capped at two consecutive
spreads.

- **A · OPENING** — number, title, year, dimensions, medium and note on
  the verso; the uncropped painting on the recto, top on the first
  register, scaled by physical size. Variants: *threshold* (the year as a
  large numeral above the title), *integrated* (a study, version or single
  detail docked to the text column), *paired* (two related minor works on
  one spread, one dominant, both whole), *monumental* (the physically
  largest canvases take the full plate band).
- **B · ARGUMENT** — one dominant crop (full-page bleed, full-height
  column, or a band across the spine where the centre is safe) against one
  to three counter-images on registers. Every crop captioned where it is
  and what the hand did there. Never a symmetric grid.
- **C · SEQUENCE** — time, not inventory: the decisive stage dominant, the
  remaining stages as a strip, a column or a stagger, in order, numbered,
  with one line saying what changed. Used for process photographs, painted
  versions, and study → painting comparisons.
- **D · PAUSE** — a sentence of the artist's, set at display size, tied to
  the image it describes; the rest of the spread is committed white. Three
  in the book, each composed differently, none reusing the first edition's
  centred-quote template.
- **E · SURVEY** — the deliberately archival pages, where small images have
  an indexical function: contents, the one-width index, the *What comes
  back* opening, and the colophon with the cover's locator.

The six motif spreads inside *What comes back* are one-off compositions on
the same grid: **the onlooker** disperses its eight witnesses across the
spread around one dominant; **the chair** isolates four crops in committed
emptiness; **the crow** ascends the spread diagonally and leaves off the
top edge; **the cage** sets its crops on drawn rules that never close;
**the body** packs its crops into one butted, cellular mass; **the face**
confronts — two faces at monumental scale across the gutter, the rest as
direct pairs. No crop there repeats a crop from the work sections.

## 5 · Eight spreads, as built

1. **Work opening + year threshold — pp. 10–11.** Verso: `2026` at 108 pt
   hanging from the first register, one line — *Six works · Luxembourg* —
   then `01`, the title, dimensions, and the note with its three facets in
   one block, white collecting at the foot. Recto: *7 fam board game*
   uncropped, 204 mm wide, top on the first register. The numeral is the
   only threshold the year gets.
2. **Complete painting — pp. 80–81.** *despite transgression*, at
   100 × 160 cm the largest canvas in the book, takes the full 260 mm plate
   band; the verso carries only title, metadata and note, set high, the
   foot left white against the painting's mass.
3. **Detail argument — pp. 18–19.** Virgil: the wigged face bleeds off the
   verso; on the recto the grey head that lies beneath the composition is
   held at 100 mm with the caption block. Above against below —
   the painting's power structure restated by scale.
4. **Process sequence — pp. 22–23.** The verso pairs Leonardo's *Virgin of
   the Rocks* (credited) with the pencil transfer under it; the recto sets
   the red drowning of the ground dominant with the remaining stages as a
   numbered strip. One line: what was taken, what was buried, what was
   assembled on top.
5. **Typographic pause — pp. 66–67.** *When the Darkness surrounds…*: the
   reaching arm enters the recto from the outer edge; across the spread,
   set in 30 pt italic serif, ragged, "it could be about to strike, it
   could be reached out to hold something." — the sentence stops short of
   the open hand. The rest of the spread stays white.
6. **Year transition — pp. 44–45.** 2023: `2023` above *penguin is not a
   friend, only a sinner*, the one commission, with the factual line on the
   painting years thinning to this canvas before 2026 resumes. The verso is
   two-thirds empty and means it; the recto carries the square painting
   alone.
7. **Recurring comparison — pp. 126–127.** *The face*: the head worked
   until it has no face (18) bleeds off the verso; the face drawn over
   itself (32) answers it on the recto; beneath, two direct pairs at one
   size — 1 against 12, 21 against 27 — every crop new to the book.
8. **Closing — pp. 128–129.** Verso: the colophon. Recto: *7 fam board
   game* printed small and whole with a hairline rectangle marking exactly
   where the cover was cut, the mark beneath. The book ends by showing
   where it began.

## The standard

Four questions are asked of every spread before it stays: what dominates;
why is it that size; what does it do to the page it faces; what does it add
that the book has not already said. `book.py` enforces what is countable —
crop uniqueness, family runs, dominance shares, register discipline — and
`audit.js` measures the rendered file. A spread that fails is rebuilt.
